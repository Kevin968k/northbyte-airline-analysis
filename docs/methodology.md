# Methodology

## Financial comparisons

Fiscal years run from September through August. The analytical financial result is net transaction revenue minus supplied operating costs and gate rent. A positive shortfall is its negative. This is not an audited net-income calculation.

Revenue uses transaction dates and operating costs use flight dates. These comparisons are fiscal-period financial results, not revenue matched to individual flown services.

Valid interpretations of ambiguous dates generate revenue bounds. Those bounds are not statistical confidence intervals. Monthly extremes cannot necessarily occur simultaneously and must not simply be summed into annual bounds.

## Network and operating costs

Opposite directions are combined into airport pairs: 54 directional routes become 27 pairs. Operating costs include fuel, crew, maintenance and food. Flight counts must count each flight once across category records. Average cost per flight equals operating cost divided by flight count.

Aggregate changes can reflect volume, prices and flight mix. They do not establish causality or marginal flight profitability.

## Gate rent

Calendar-year leases are converted to fiscal years using four months of the starting calendar year and eight months of the following calendar year. Low, Base and High scenarios address missing 2026 rent.

Monthly airport rent is allocated across distinct departing flights and then aggregated to routes. Unallocated rent remains in company totals. Allocated rent is not automatically avoidable, and missing route assignments do not prove a gate is unused.

## Frequency scenario

For each priority pair, let N be annual flights, n removed flights, C annual operating cost, R annual net revenue, and a the assumed avoidable cost share:

`modelled improvement = a × (n / N) × C − (n / N) × R`

The scenario removes approximately 10% of flights, rounding down to an even number for paired removals. This does not establish a feasible timetable. Gate rent is held constant. The 40%, 60% and 80% shares are assumptions, not probabilities or validated cost estimates.

Implementation costs, revenue recapture and network effects are excluded. These omissions can affect results in opposite directions, so the model should not be described as uniformly conservative.

## Decision use

Historical results prioritize investigations. A real schedule decision requires specific flights, validated cost flexibility, revenue and connection impacts, implementation costs, and operating constraints. The proposed 90-day sequence is validation, controlled pilots and review; annualized model results are not 90-day cash-flow forecasts.
